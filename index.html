VeriLiquid

Thomas Oberlin co-founded MightyGreens, Brazil's first urban mushroom farm, with supermarket contracts and real demand. It nearly didn't survive its own growth. The lenders could see the purchase orders — the problem was that rapid expansion meant the capital required always outpaced the sales volume coming back. Substrate purchased ninety days before a single mushroom could be sold. Add the fruiting cycle. Add delivery. Add the supermarket's forty-five-day payment terms. The business was sound. The timing was brutal. Global trade credit markets are tightening further, making this gap worse, not better.

The data existed. The cash cycle didn't care.

That is the problem VeriLiquid solves — and it isn't unique to MightyGreens. A typical SME food importer has more than €500K permanently tied up in a single month's shipment cycle. VeriPura's compliance attestation record makes that risk legible for the first time. VeriLiquid deploys capital against it — a tokenised receivables facility using blockchain-based tokenisation of trade receivables, enabling importers to access working capital at rates no traditional lender can offer without our data. This is Act 2.
